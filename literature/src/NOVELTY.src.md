# Novelty assessment against the literature

A population assembly catalogue of human subtelomeres already exists as a preprint ([[10.64898/2026.09.03.748401]]). It reports, from 860 haploid assemblies, that most autosomal arms carry several terminal haplotypes differing by up to 100 to 200 kb, that the shared blocks are conserved in great apes, that block diversity follows arm rather than ancestry, and that the haplotypes differ in gene content. Earlier optical-map surveys had catalogued long-range subtelomere haplotypes in 154 genomes ([[10.1371/journal.pgen.1008347]]). The atlas is therefore not the first population catalogue of subtelomere structure, and multi-haplotype arms, ape conservation and gene-content differences should be presented as confirmations.

The atlas contributes six things not available elsewhere:

1. A validated allele-level catalogue of terminal replacements, with switch points shared base-for-base between samples. Catalogue v0.1 revision 2 has {{cat_alleles}} structural alleles (tiers A/B/C = {{cat_tier_A}}/{{cat_tier_B}}/{{cat_tier_C}}) and {{cat_haplogroups}} haplogroups over {{cat_ends_r2}} Release 2 ends plus CHM13 and HG002 Q100. Near-identical CHM13 ends are grouped into {{cat_families}} paralog families, and each end and allele has a nativity class.
2. Replication of the common terminal alleles in four independent assembly cohorts.
3. Evidence that these terminal blocks arise by one-way, homology-mediated transfer.
4. Evidence on the origin and age of common forms. Carriers form tight clusters for most testable forms ({{age_forms_clustered_crit}} of {{age_forms_analysed}}), but single origin is supported for only {{age_single_origin}} of {{age_forms_analysed}}. Clock ages of clustered forms are hundreds of kyr, older than the out-of-Africa dispersal.
5. A negative test of cis telomere-length effects, in which the window artefact behind an earlier positive association is explained.
6. A validated query tool.

Status definitions: **established** means replicated by independent groups or methods; **contested** means published claims conflict or rest on a single unvalidated analysis; **unknown** means no study had tested it in humans before the atlas. Atlas values come from [evidence_numbers.json](evidence_numbers.json), generated by `scripts/evidence_numbers.py` from the final track outputs (`analyses/telomere_cis`, `analyses/junctions`, `analyses/allele_ages`), the catalogue (`data/catalogue`, v0.1 revision 2), the cross-check against Tan et al. 2026 (`data/validation`), the query-tool validation (`tests/validation`) and the analysis home. Two sets of counts appear together below. The analysis tracks (telomere length, junctions, ages) used the earlier per-window form calls, labelled by the single best CHM13 end (for example 20q>3q). The catalogue counts use revision 2, which labels foreign blocks by paralog family (for example 20q>PF04, with PF04 = 3q and 19p). Their form and foreign-end counts therefore differ. A "junction" or "switch" in the atlas is the position at which the terminal sequence changes which CHM13 end it most resembles. It lies within paralogous sequence shared by both arms and is a change in paralog similarity, not necessarily a structural breakpoint.

## Implications for the NEW_BIOLOGY report

Five statements can be presented as new findings:

1. Common terminal-replacement alleles form by one-way, homology-mediated transfer without reciprocal products.
2. Carriers form tight clusters for most testable forms ({{age_forms_clustered_crit}} of {{age_forms_analysed}}), and single origin is supported for {{age_single_origin}} of {{age_forms_analysed}}. Clock ages of clustered forms are hundreds of kyr, older than the out-of-Africa dispersal. The specific human switch configurations are absent from great apes.
3. The foreign or own state of the terminal block does not detectably change the adjacent telomere's length (no effect at or above about {{tl_S20_mde}} bp). An earlier positive association was a window artefact.
4. The common terminal alleles replicate in four independent cohorts: all {{tan_rep_n}} common tier-A alleles recur, and frequencies of non-native alleles agree (r = {{tan_freq_r}}).
5. These alleles are now available as a validated, queryable catalogue.

The prevalence of foreign terminal blocks, which depends on the window used, the existence of several terminal haplotypes per arm, their gene-content differences and the conservation of shared blocks in apes should be presented as confirmations of earlier and concurrent work.
