# Human subtelomeres: a structured literature review

Human chromosome ends are mosaics of duplicated blocks shared among non-homologous arms, and three decades of locus-level work established that these blocks move between ends, that whole terminal segments can be replaced, and that telomere length is regulated in part by the end on which a telomere sits. What the literature has not settled is how often terminal replacement alleles arise, whether they form by reciprocal or one-way transfer, how old they are, and whether exchange is ongoing at rates visible in pedigrees. Population-scale assembly catalogues addressing these questions appeared only in 2026, as preprints ([Guarracino et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.10.737660); [Tan et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.03.748401)). This review covers 199 included records, selected from 1,485 retrieved candidates (44 graded as directly relevant); the search strategy is in [search_log.json](search_log.json) and the per-record annotations are in [records.tsv](records.tsv).

## Architecture and exchange

### Duplicon patchworks at chromosome ends

The defining feature of a human subtelomere is a patchwork of segmental duplications whose copies sit near the ends of several unrelated chromosomes ([Mefford & Trask 2002](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrg727)). Early cloning of telomere-associated DNA already showed repeat families shared among ends and allelic polymorphism next to the TTAGGG tract ([Brown et al. 1990](https://doi.org/10.1016/0092-8674%2890%2990293-n); [de Lange et al. 1990](https://doi.org/10.1128/mcb.10.2.518)). Sequence-level work then resolved a recurring two-part organisation: a distal domain of short, interrupted homologies to many ends and a proximal domain of 10 to 40 kb blocks shared with few ends, often separated by degenerate internal (TTAGGG)n tracts ([Flint 1997](https://doi.org/10.1093/hmg/6.8.1305); [Flint et al. 1997](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng0397-252)). Reference-era assemblies of the 41 distinct telomere regions quantified the patchwork ([Riethman et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.1245004)), and duplicon analysis showed that subtelomeric repeats make up most of the distal 100 kb and that a small set of subterminal duplicon families accounts for all telomere-adjacent sequence then known ([Ambrosini et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1186/gb-2007-8-7-r151)). Gap-filled assemblies with a multiread annotation pipeline were the last reference-based update before complete genomes ([Stong et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.166983.113)).

These blocks are polymorphic in presence, copy number and location. An olfactory-receptor-containing block is fixed at three ends but present polymorphically at eleven others, with frequencies that differ among populations ([Trask et al. 1998](https://doi.org/10.1093/hmg/7.1.13)), and SNP analysis of its copies shows recent exchange between non-homologous ends ([Mefford 2001](https://doi.org/10.1093/hmg/10.21.2363)). The 16p tip carries discrete length alleles differing by up to 260 kb that segregate as stable Mendelian variants ([Wilkie et al. 1991](https://doi.org/10.1016/0092-8674%2891%2990243-r)). FISH surveys with subtelomeric probes found widespread segmental polymorphism consistent with both reciprocal translocation and homology-driven spreading ([Der-Sarkissian et al. 2002](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.322802); [Ijdo et al. 1992](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0888-7543%2805%2980125-9)). The clearest early case of a transposed terminal allele is a telomere plus adjacent DNA present on a minority of 16p and 16q chromosomes, which must have relocated together with its telomere-adjacent sequence ([Coleman et al. 1999](https://doi.org/10.1093/hmg/8.9.1637)).

Comparative data place much of this turnover in recent primate history. About half of the known subtelomeric sequence was inferred to be human-specific, formed through repeated translocations between ends ([Linardopoulou et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature04029)). The 15q end, created by a fission in the great-ape ancestor, has since exchanged material with other subtelomeres, including a transfer of at least 200 kb to 4q in African apes ([Rudd et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.083170.108)). Complete ape genomes now show lineage-specific subterminal satellite caps with independent origins in Pan and gorilla ([Ventura et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.136556.111); [Yoo et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.280987.125); [Yoo et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08816-3)) and distinct subtelomeric satellite architectures in macaque ([Zhang et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.02.018)). Yeast long-read assemblies show the same pattern of subtelomeres as the fastest-rearranging compartment of the genome ([Yue et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng.3847)).

### Mechanisms of terminal transfer

Two mechanistic models compete. The first is reciprocal or non-homologous exchange: breakpoint analysis of human subtelomeric duplications concluded that non-homologous end-joining predominates in creating them, with homology-based transfers acting on blocks once duplicated ([Linardopoulou et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature04029)). The second is one-way copying of a donor end onto a broken recipient. In yeast, a one-ended break invades a distant chromosome end with as little as 72 bp of homology and copies it to the telomere by break-induced replication (BIR), producing a nonreciprocal translocation and telomere capture ([Bosco & Haber 1998](https://doi.org/10.1093/genetics/150.3.1037)). BIR is now well characterised as a conservative, mutagenic, template-switching process ([Llorente et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.4161/cc.7.7.5613); [Kramara et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tig.2018.04.002); [Lydeard et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature06047)), whose microhomology-mediated form explains complex human CNVs with 2 to 5 bp junction microhomology ([Hastings et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1000327)). Whether a subtelomeric break is repaired by gene conversion or BIR depends on its distance from the end and on resection and chromatin ([Batté et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.15252/embj.201796631)).

Clinical cytogenetics supplies direct human examples of one-way terminal replacement. Apparent terminal deletions are often cryptic subtelomeric translocations stabilised by capture of another chromosome's telomere ([Meltzer et al. 1993](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng0793-252)), and capture is frequent among inverted duplication-deletions and 1p36 derivatives ([Yu & Graf 2010](https://doi.org/10.1159/000315887); [Kostiner et al. 2002](https://doi.org/10.1159/000068536); [Ballif et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00439-003-1029-y)). The 1p36 breakpoints lack homology or low-copy repeats, which pointed to BIR rather than non-allelic homologous recombination ([Ballif et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00439-003-1029-y)). The alternative outcome, direct telomere healing by de novo TTAGGG addition, was first shown on a truncated 16p ([Wilkie et al. 1990](https://doi.org/10.1038/346868a0)), and its frequency relative to interstitial telomere retention has been surveyed in constitutional rearrangements ([Fortin et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1159/000230002)). A 16p break stabilised by recombination between two related Alu elements, one of them subtelomeric, is the closest human precedent for a homology-mediated terminal replacement in the germline ([Flint 1996](https://doi.org/10.1093/hmg/5.8.1163)). Junction sequencing of pathogenic subtelomeric rearrangements found a mix of end-joining, repeat-mediated recombination and replication-based signatures ([Luo et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1093/hmg/ddr293)), and long-read assemblies have begun to assign repair signatures to large population subtelomeric SVs ([Kim et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkae1294)). Mitotic sister chromatid exchange is also strongly elevated at chromosome ends ([Rudd et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.0030032)).

### Interstitial telomeric sequences at junctions

Short internal TTAGGG-like tracts are enriched more than 25-fold in subtelomeres ([Riethman et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.1245004)) and sit at duplicon boundaries, which led to the proposal that they mark old junctions ([Ambrosini et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1186/gb-2007-8-7-r151)). Human ITSs fall into short, subtelomeric and fusion classes ([Azzalin et al. 2001](https://doi.org/10.1007/s004120100135)). Comparative primate analysis shows short ITSs were inserted suddenly at double-strand-break repair sites rather than grown by expansion ([Nergadze et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.2778904)), possibly via telomerase ([Nergadze et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1186/gb-2007-8-12-r260)), whereas the 2q13 fusion ITS records an ancestral telomere-telomere fusion ([IJdo et al. 1991](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.88.20.9051); [Fan et al. 2002](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.337602)). ITSs are length-polymorphic and unstable ([Mondello et al. 2000](https://doi.org/10.1006/geno.2000.6280); [Aksenova & Mirkin 2019](https://doi.org/10.3390/genes10020118); [Ruiz-Herrera et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1159/000167807)). In yeast, BIR that runs into an ITS frequently terminates there and seeds an ectopic telomere ([Stivison et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkaa1081)), and ITSs raise gross rearrangement rates through several pathways ([Rosas Bringas et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2407314121)). This gives a testable prediction: if terminal replacements arise by BIR-like copying, junctions should carry donor homology and sometimes ITS remnants.

### Model systems: 4q/10q, PAR1, acrocentrics

The 4q/10q system is the best-dated subtelomere exchange. Exchange of D4Z4 units between 4q35 and 10q26 was inferred from Southern data ([van Deutekom 1996](https://doi.org/10.1093/hmg/5.12.1997)), translocated arrays were found in a fifth of Dutch donors ([Overveld 2000](https://doi.org/10.1093/hmg/9.19.2879)), and translocation frequencies vary by population ([Matsumura et al. 2002](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2377-2-7)). Haplotype analysis in HapMap and HGDP panels then showed that all 4q and 10q subtelomeres trace to only four discrete transfer events, all present before the out-of-Africa dispersal ([Lemmers et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajhg.2010.01.035)). This is the main precedent for treating subtelomere exchange alleles as few and old rather than recurrent. The 4qA and 4qB distal variants, and the permissive haplotype required for FSHD, show that such alleles have phenotypic consequences ([Lemmers et al. 2002](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng999); [Lemmers et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1189044)), and engineered 4q/10q exchange reverts the disease genotype ([Ma et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adl1922)). T2T-era work warns that D4Z4-like repeats extend beyond these two loci ([Salsi et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41431-025-02000-x)).

PAR1 is the one human terminal region with obligatory meiotic exchange, with a male crossover rate far above the genome average ([Helena Mangs & Morris 2007](https://doi.org/10.2174/138920207780368141); [Hinch et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1004503)). Its boundary is itself polymorphic: an X-to-Y insertional translocation creates an extended PAR1 in a subset of men ([Mensah et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1004578); [Poriswanish et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1007680)), and the boundary position remains debated ([Bellott et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajhg.2024.09.005); [Cotter et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1534/genetics.114.172692)). The Xp/Yp telomere junction carries a few highly diverged haplotypes in strong linkage disequilibrium ([Baird et al. 1995](https://doi.org/10.1002/j.1460-2075.1995.tb00227.x); [Baird 1997](https://doi.org/10.1093/hmg/6.13.2291)).

The acrocentric short arms are the precedent for pangenome inference of heterologous exchange: shared pseudo-homologous regions in assemblies were interpreted as evidence of ongoing recombination between non-homologous p-arms ([Guarracino et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-05976-y)). A four-generation pedigree later found allelic p-arm recombination depleted and a single ectopic event in 107 transmissions ([Lin et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.05.035)), a calibration that population inference alone cannot supply.

## Telomere length at individual ends

### Arm-specific and allele-specific length

That telomere length depends on the chromosome end is established. Q-FISH revealed large length heterogeneity among telomeres within a cell ([Lansdorp 1996](https://doi.org/10.1093/hmg/5.5.685)) and consistently short 17p telomeres ([Martens et al. 1998](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng0198-018)); homologues differ in length ([Londono-Vallejo 2001](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/29.15.3164)), relative lengths of individual telomeres are fixed in the zygote ([Graakjaer et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1474-9728.2004.00093.x)), and allelic relative lengths segregate in families ([Graakjaer et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00439-006-0137-x)). STELA demonstrated extensive allelic length variation ([Baird et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng1084)) and, across 2p, 11q, 12q, 17p and XpYp, end-specific erosion and stability that hTERT overexpression erases, the basis for the claim of cis-acting determinants ([Britt-Compton et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1093/hmg/ddi486)). Long-read methods confirmed end-specific lengths conserved across individuals ([Karimian et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.ado0431)), with arm explaining about 9% of length variance in more than 2,500 All of Us participants ([Jain et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-71172-x)), across tissues and populations ([Engel et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.64898/2025.12.10.693405)) and across 232 HPRC individuals ([Li et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.19.745664)), and trio studies show inheritance of arm-level length with parent-of-origin effects ([Zhou et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.10.07.680721); [Cechova et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.64898/2025.12.14.693655)).

The candidate cis determinant has shifted from the subtelomere to the telomere-proximal variant repeats. Heritable telomere variant sequences explain much of allele-specific length, and deleting them resets length ([Chai et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77310-9)); a 212-individual atlas finds a structured, heritable variant-repeat code at nearly every end, modulated by subtelomeric TAR1 elements ([Dubocanin et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.01.722200)). In yeast, subtelomeric heterochromatin sets a telomere-specific length that moves with the distal 15 kb to another end ([Teplitz et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkaf1457)). Evidence that the subtelomere allele itself sets length in humans is weak. Optical mapping proposed haplotype-linked length measurement but did not test it ([Young et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkx017)). The 860-assembly catalogue found only suggestive associations between terminal haplotype and length at 1p, 5q and 6q (FDR < 0.1, all other arms non-significant) ([Tan et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.03.748401)). The atlas paired test of foreign versus own terminal alleles within individuals finds no positive cis effect: boundary-robust estimates are -133 to +14 bp (all p ≥ 0.36). A positive association obtained with a telomere-length-dependent classification window was an artefact of that window ([NOVELTY.md](NOVELTY.md)).

### Long-read measurement methods

Chromosome-end-resolved length is now routine from long reads. Telogator anchors reads to subtelomere references ([Stephens et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btac005)), and Telogator2 uses variant repeats to separate all 92 alleles ([Stephens & Kocher 2024](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12859-024-05807-5)). Capture-based PacBio HiFi ([Tham et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-35823-7)), nanopore Telomere Profiling ([Karimian et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.ado0431)) and Telo-seq ([Schmidt et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-48917-7)) give single-telomere resolution, with additional pipelines and benchmarks appearing ([Nguyen & Choi 2025](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-025-03783-4); [Deimler et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1013915); [Reed et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.01.29.635538)). A known pitfall is systematic nanopore miscalling of telomeric repeats ([Tan et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-022-02751-6)), and every read-assignment method depends on the subtelomere reference used, so mis-specified terminal alleles propagate into length calls.

## Resources and theory

### Complete genomes and pangenomes

T2T-CHM13 provided the first complete set of human ends ([Nurk et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abj6987)) together with a complete segmental duplication map ([Vollger et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abj6965)). The HG002 Q100 diploid benchmark adds a second, independently validated pair of haplotypes ([Hansen et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.06.016)). HPRC Release 1 delivered 47 phased diploid assemblies ([Liao et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-05896-x)) and Release 2 expanded this about fivefold ([Lucas et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.21.739710)), built with hifiasm and Verkko ([Cheng et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-020-01056-5); [Rautiainen et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-023-01662-6)). HGSVC3 and population SD surveys extend complete-region SV analysis ([Logsdon et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09140-6); [Jeong et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-024-02051-8)). Assembly breaks and misjoins concentrate in SD-rich regions ([Porubsky et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.277334.122)), so subtelomeric calls from assemblies require orthogonal read-level and pedigree validation. Multi-generation T2T pedigrees now allow direct observation of transmission ([Porubsky et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08922-2); [Cechova et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.64898/2025.12.14.693655)).

### Existing subtelomere catalogues

Before complete assemblies, subtelomere resources were clone sets for clinical FISH ([Ning et al. 1996](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng0996-86); [Knight et al. 2000](https://doi.org/10.1086/302998)) and reference assemblies ([Riethman et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.1245004); [Stong et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.166983.113)). The first population catalogue of long-range subtelomere haplotypes used optical maps of 154 genomes from 26 populations and estimated a timeline for duplicon spread ([Young et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1008347); [Young et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkx017)), and targeted assembly methods followed ([Adam et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12859-022-05081-3)). In 2026, three assembly-scale preprints appeared. One compares all ends across 465 assemblies and groups them into sequence communities ([Guarracino et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.10.737660)). A second releases complete subtelomere sequences for all non-acrocentric arms from 860 haploid assemblies, reporting multiple haplotypes differing by 100 to 200 kb on most autosomal arms, arm-not-ancestry structure, conservation of blocks in great apes, and rare recombination ([Tan et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.03.748401)), with sequences deposited publicly ([Tan 2026](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21807155)). A third catalogues telomere arrays rather than subtelomeres ([Dubocanin et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.01.722200)). An assembly-based population catalogue of human subtelomere structure therefore already exists in preprint form, including multi-haplotype arms, conservation of shared blocks in great apes and gene-content differences between haplotypes. A new atlas must define its contribution against it. One caution applies to every such catalogue: the fraction of ends called foreign depends on how much terminal sequence is classified. In the atlas it is 49.8%, 41.1% and 34.2% for the first 5, 10 and 20 kb after the telomere boundary.

### Concerted evolution as a frame

The 2026 subtelomere preprint interprets shared terminal sequence as concerted evolution driven by recurrent ectopic exchange ([Guarracino et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.10.737660)). The concept comes from repeated-DNA theory: unequal exchange and gene conversion homogenise copies so that a family evolves as a unit ([Smith 1976](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1251186); [Dover 1982](https://doi.org/10.1038/299111a0); [Ohta 1983](https://doi.org/10.1016/0040-5809%2883%2990015-1); [Elder, & Turner 1995](https://doi.org/10.1086/419073); [Liao 1999](https://doi.org/10.1086/302221)). Interlocus gene conversion is measurable in human SDs ([Dumont & Eichler 2013](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0075949); [Vollger et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-05895-y)). The competing model, birth-and-death evolution, predicts discrete copies created by duplication that persist or are lost independently ([Nei & Rooney 2005](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.genet.39.073003.112240)). The two make different predictions for subtelomeres: concerted exchange predicts recurrent, independently formed junctions and frequent de novo events, whereas birth-and-death predicts a small number of old alleles with shared junctions that are transmitted intact. The 4q/10q data fit the second pattern ([Lemmers et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajhg.2010.01.035)), and the RNU2 array shows that a repeat can evolve concertedly almost entirely within a chromosome lineage, with interchromosomal exchange rare ([Liao et al. 1997](https://doi.org/10.1093/emboj/16.3.588)).

### Where the atlas stands

The atlas resolves several of the open points above; details and statistics are in [NOVELTY.md](NOVELTY.md).

On mechanism, the donor ends of carriers are no more often reciprocal than those of non-carriers (3.86% vs 3.91%, p = 0.88). Of 383 switch clusters, 85.0% lie inside donor-recipient homology. This favours one-way, homology-directed copying over the end-joining-dominated model ([Linardopoulou et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature04029)). Interstitial telomeric sequences are not enriched at switches more than 5 kb from the telomere.

On age, carriers form tight clusters for most testable forms (23 of 34; 27 of 61 selected forms could not be tested), but single origin is supported for only 13 of 34, and none is strictly monophyletic. Median clock ages are 0.2 to 1.5 Myr for 17 of 23 clustered forms, and none of 70 recurrent switch configurations is present in the T2T great-ape genomes. This is consistent with the 4q/10q picture of few, old transfers ([Lemmers et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajhg.2010.01.035)) for a subset of forms; it does not establish single origin genome-wide.

On telomere length, the terminal allele does not detectably change the adjacent telomere's length, and pedigree transmission is overwhelmingly intact (51 of 54 foreign ends).

On reproducibility, the revision-2 catalogue (647 structural alleles, 593 haplogroups) agrees with the Tan et al. sequences of the same haplotypes for 96.4% of alleles. All 88 common tier-A alleles recur in four independent cohorts ([Logsdon et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09140-6); [Gao et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06173-7)), and non-native allele frequencies there match Release 2 (r = 0.971). The mechanism, age and telomere-length analyses used the earlier per-window form calls, whereas the catalogue labels foreign blocks by paralog family, so form counts differ between the two.

Two caveats bound these results. Atlas switch points are changes in paralog similarity within shared sequence, so they do not pin down structural breakpoints. Gene conversion can blur both single-origin calls and ages.
