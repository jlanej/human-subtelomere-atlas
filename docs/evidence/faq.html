FAQ for skeptics

Alternative explanations for a read that looks like a non-native terminal block, and how each is excluded. The full discussion is in READ_EVIDENCE.md.

Is this not just a chimeric read?

A chimera joins two molecules. A definitive read has no internal telomere, no adapter sequence, no foldback, and at least 90% of its subtelomeric part aligned. The switch it shows also falls, within ± 2 kb, where the person's own assembly places it. Chimeras form at random positions; they do not reproduce the same switch in independent molecules, datasets and platforms (HG002: up to 9 datasets per claim), or in the parent who transmitted the haplotype.

Could the reads come from a different chromosome end (mis-mapping, paralogs)?

This is the main risk, and the reason the definitive tier exists. Diagnostic units say which CHM13 end a stretch most resembles, but human haplotypes carry blocks that CHM13 lacks. For example, 6p, 20q and 9q haplotypes carry an 11p-like tip followed by 3q-like sequence (DIAGNOSTICS.md). A definitive read must therefore also (i) anchor with ≥ 1 kb in CHM13 sequence unique to the recipient end and (ii) align best to the claimed end window among all of the same person's assembly end windows. Reads that fail (ii) are reported as places on another end.

Could the assembly be wrong?

The read test is assembly-free: the units come from CHM13 alone, and the reads were not used to build the HPRC Release 2 assemblies. The 1000 Genomes ONT reads come from another laboratory and platform (R9.4.1), and the GIAB parental reads from other DNA samples. Assemblies enter only to locate the expected switch (concordance) and in the post-hoc placement step.

What about population variation at the diagnostic sites?

Carrier haplotypes differ from CHM13 at some diagnostic sites, so purity thresholds are 0.80, not 1.0 (true-carrier donor purity is as low as 0.80; calibration T0). Variation of this kind lowers purity in both segments. It does not create an ordered donor-then-recipient pattern with log10 likelihood ratios ≥ 5 against both no-switch models. In strictly native controls, 0 of 1,010 read × model tests (1KG-ONT) and 0 of 297 HG002 other-haplotype tests were conclusive.

Are the reads independent?

HG002 reads are de-duplicated by molecule across datasets (same ONT read UUID, or PacBio movie and ZMW). Corrected (HERRO) reads are derived from the ultra-long reads; they are shown but never counted. 12 of 15 definitive HG002 claims have definitive reads from ≥ 2 independent datasets.

Were the thresholds tuned to get these results?

The v1 criteria were registered and frozen (hash a89c5eaf023ed032) on HG002 training ends and native controls before any population read was fetched (CRITERIA.md, CALIBRATION.md). The definitive tier was defined later. It can only remove claims, and both tiers are reported for every claim.

Why are most population claims not assessable?

Reads must reach the telomere and extend past the switch by ≥ 10 kb. 1KG-ONT R9.4.1 reads are shorter than HG002 ultra-long reads, and the telomere-reaching subset is small. A claim without such a read cannot be tested. That is not evidence against it, and it is labelled not assessable with the reason in claims.tsv.

Why are some confirmed claims not definitive?

19 of 43 confirmed 1KG-ONT claims rest on reads whose anchor lies only in CHM13-shared sequence (e.g. 2q SA-2q-02, whose outer ~100+ kb are shared), or on reads that align best to another end of the same person, often the other haplotype at the same arm. They remain v1-confirmed. The definitive tier does not resolve them.

Which arms lack controls?

Nine claimed arms have no strictly native individual among the 42 (12p, 17q, 19p, 19q, 1p, 3q, 4q, 6q, Yq). For these arms, specificity rests on HG002 other-haplotype controls and the calibration.

How do I check one read myself?

Every read in reads.tsv.gz has the exact samtools command that prints it from the public file, plus the region and read group. The showcase directories have CRAM/BAM files and IGV sessions. See Verify it yourself.
