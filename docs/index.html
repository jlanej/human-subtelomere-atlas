Human subtelomere atlas

A validated, queryable catalogue of the structural alleles at human chromosome ends. It describes the terminal 100 kb next to every telomere in 20,144 telomeric contig ends from HPRC Release 2 (230 samples), the 92 ends of the HG002 Q100 assembly and the 48 ends of T2T-CHM13 v2.0, as mosaics of blocks labelled home, by paralog family or by the CHM13 arm they resemble most.

New here? Start with one molecule. A single 513 kb DNA molecule from HG002 starts in the telomere, reads like chromosome 11 for 31 kb, then switches and runs along chromosome 3 (MAPQ 60). Every one of the 230 Release 2 people carries foreign chromosome ends (median 21 of about 90 assembled ends). See the molecule · two-minute PDF

Concurrent work on the same question: Tan et al. 2026 (doi:10.64898/2026.09.03.748401) and Guarracino et al. 2026 (doi:10.64898/2026.07.10.737660); this atlas is complementary to both (details).

How blocks are labelled (catalogue revision 2). At every 100 bp point of an end's subtelomere, the identity of the surrounding 5 kb to each of the 48 CHM13 v2.0 ends is read from one alignment of the whole window.

  • Home rule. A point is home if a CHM13 copy of the end's own arm (or of an arm in its pooled group) is within 0.5 percentage points of the best identity, so home wins ties.
  • Paralog families. Some CHM13 ends share long, near-identical duplicated blocks, so the single best end can flip between them. Ends that are tied with each other at ≥ 0.75 of the points where the less often tied one is tied form a family; a non-home block is labelled with the family of its best end, otherwise with that end's arm. Families: PF01 = 4p, 14p, 15p, 21p, 22p; PF02 = 1p, 5q, 6q; PF03 = 11p, 15q; PF04 = 3q, 19p; PF05 = Xp, Yp; PF06 = Xq, Yq. The best single CHM13 end of each block is kept as best_end.
  • none. No CHM13 end within 0.80 identity: sequence without a close CHM13 counterpart (neither home nor foreign evidence).
  • Nativity class of a core structure: native = only home (and none) blocks; within family = every non-home block is in a family that contains the end's own arm; foreign = at least one block from another family or arm.
Terminology. A switch (junction) is a change in which CHM13 end the sequence most resembles; it lies inside shared, paralogous sequence and is not necessarily a structural breakpoint. All labels are relative to the CHM13 reference.

Key findings

Finding 1 uses catalogue revision 2. Findings 2-4 come from the three analyses, which used the earlier per-window best-end form calls (e.g. 20q>3q); their counts are not directly comparable with revision-2 S20 or nativity counts (definitions and concordance table).

  1. Non-native terminal sequence is common. Of 20,144 Release 2 ends, 4,936 (24.5%) have a foreign and 747 (3.7%) a within-family core structure; the first 20 kb are mostly foreign in 17.4% (S20 state). The fraction depends on the window and on the labelling rule: the earlier per-window best-end calls gave 49.8%, 41.1% and 34.2% for the first 5, 10 and 20 kb of 11,998 chromosome-named tips.
  2. Common forms are old, recurrent structural alleles with independent validation. For example, SA-20q-01 (structure PF04|home: 20q carrying a terminal block of paralog family PF04 (3q, 19p), best single end 3q; median switch 31.5 kb from the telomere boundary) is carried by 146 samples (Release 2 frequency 0.4829; tier A). In the analyses, unrelated people share base-identical switch points: 34 of 39 junction clusters with ≥ 10 samples contain both continental African and non-African samples. Median-based clock ages of 17 of 23 clustered forms fall between 0.2 and 1.5 Myr (median 502 kyr); these ages depend on the mutation-rate and genealogy model. None of the 70 recurrent junctions tested was found in the T2T great-ape genomes (0 candidates after all 31 probe hits were diagnosed as shared duplicons); absence is weak evidence, because orthology of ape chromosome ends could be assigned only in part.
  3. The transfer is one-way and homology-mediated. The donor arm's end in carrier haplotypes looks like that of non-carriers (reciprocal configuration 3.86% vs 3.91%; chi-square p = 0.88), and 327 of 383 junction clusters (85%) lie within donor-recipient homology. Interstitial telomeric sequence is not a general feature of junctions.
  4. The terminal allele does not set telomere length in cis. Arm-specific telomere lengths in the assemblies reproduce published nanopore measurements (Spearman ρ = 0.73, 38 arms), but foreign and own terminal alleles in the same individual do not differ in telomere length; an earlier positive association was an artefact of a telomere-length-dependent classification window.

Read-level evidence. Single long reads that were not used for the assemblies span the telomere, the claimed switch and a unique recipient anchor: all 15 confirmed HG002 Q100 claims are definitive (12 with reads from ≥ 2 independent datasets), the transmitting parent confirms 4, and 24 of 43 confirmed 1000 Genomes ONT claims are definitive; no negative-control read was conclusive (0 of 297 and 0 of 1,010 tests). Relation to concurrent work

This atlas is not the first population catalogue of human subtelomeres. It complements two concurrent preprints:

  • Tan et al. 2026, Widespread structural variations at human chromosome ends, bioRxiv, doi:10.64898/2026.09.03.748401: complete subtelomeres from 860 haplotypes over 500 kb, organised into paralogy-block haplotypes; sequences released under CC BY 4.0 (doi:10.5281/zenodo.21807156).
  • Guarracino et al. 2026, Concerted evolution and unorthodox recombination of human subtelomeres, bioRxiv, doi:10.64898/2026.07.10.737660: all-vs-all comparison of chromosome ends in near-complete assemblies, grouping pseudo-homologous regions into sequence communities and reporting candidate subtelomere recombination in a pedigree.

The atlas contributes per-end membership for every HPRC Release 2 telomeric tip, telomere-boundary-anchored structural alleles labelled by paralog family with nativity classes, sequence haplogroups with explicit evidence tiers (Verkko, read and pedigree support), boundary-shift stability tests, representative sequences, frequencies by superpopulation, a query tool, and hypothesis tests of mechanism, age and telomere-length effects. A same-haplotype cross-check against the independent Tan et al. sequences is on the validation page; see the literature review for a fuller comparison.
