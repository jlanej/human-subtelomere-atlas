Findings › Population distribution, origin and age

Common forms are old and shared across populations

Definitions: this analysis used the earlier per-window best-end form calls (e.g. 20q>3q); its counts are not directly comparable with catalogue revision-2 S20, form or nativity counts. See the concordance table.

Full methods, statistics and reproduction instructions: analyses/allele_ages/README.md (results tables in analyses/allele_ages/results/; reproduce with bash analyses/allele_ages/run.sh).

1. Population distribution (scripts/pop_distribution.py)

Cohort. 455 haplotypes from 230 samples: AFR 138 haplotypes / 69 samples; AMR 86/44; EAS 102/51; EUR 59/30; SAS 70/36. No parent–child pairs are in the set.

Frequency definition. Carrier frequency is the number of carrier haplotypes over haplotypes with a named tip on the own arm (Wilson 95% CIs; results/form_freq_by_superpop.tsv).

Tests. 104 forms with ≥10 carriers were tested (results/form_popdiff_tests.tsv):

  • chi-square statistic with permutation of superpopulation labels among samples (10,000 permutations, seed 20261015);
  • the same permutation stratified by HiFi chemistry batch, because chemistry differs between superpopulations;
  • Fisher's exact test, AFR vs non-AFR;
  • haploid ANOVA FST (Weir 1996) and Hudson FST (Bhatia et al. 2013, doi:10.1101/gr.154831.113);
  • Benjamini–Hochberg correction within each test family.

Results.

  • 41 of 104 forms differ among superpopulations at q < 0.05 (36 with chemistry-stratified permutation).
  • 31 forms differ AFR vs non-AFR: 22 are AFR-enriched and 9 AFR-depleted.
  • Median 5-population FST is 0.023; the maximum is 0.22.
  • 66 of 104 forms occur in all five superpopulations.
  • Most differentiated forms (FST about 0.2):
    • 5q>6q: AFR 0.53, other superpopulations 0.11–0.16.
    • 4q>15p: AFR 0.07, other superpopulations 0.42–0.61.
    • 20q>3q: AFR 0.72, EAS 0.18.
  • AFR haplotypes carry more foreign tips overall: 0.438 of named tips, against 0.348–0.369 elsewhere.

Junction clusters (results/junction_cluster_superpop.tsv). These are base-resolved switches of one form within 1 kb of each other.

  • 76 of 191 clusters with ≥2 samples contain both a continental African sample (AFR excluding the admixed ACB/ASW) and an EUR/EAS/SAS sample. Random labelling would give 104.5, so clusters are population-structured.
  • Among clusters with ≥10 samples, 34 of 39 span this split.

Sharing the same base-resolved junction across the out-of-Africa split implies an origin before that split, or later gene flow.

Single origin and relative age: results

Coverage. Of 61 forms with ≥19 carrier tips, 34 could be analysed. The other 27 were skipped:

  • 26 had less than 3 kb of foreign block after the telomere in the bottom carrier quintile. Short blocks and 5 kb window-resolved switches account for this (e.g. 1p>6q, 19p>11p, 2q>16q, 5q>6q).
  • 4q>15p had only 6 native 15p alleles, because 15p is acrocentric.

Clustering. Carriers are each other's nearest haplotypes far more often than chance: median nearest-neighbour carrier fraction is 0.95, against 0.14 expected. This is significant for 33 of 34 forms (p ≤ 0.001); the exception is acrocentric 14p>22p.

Ages.

  • Median-based ages of 17 of the 23 clustered forms fall between 0.2 and 1.5 Myr (median 502 kyr).
  • Mean-based ages are higher (median 1.7 Myr for the 13 single-origin forms), because a minority of carriers are divergent.

Out-of-Africa pattern. Carrier diversity is higher among AFR carriers than among non-AFR carriers for 23 of 33 forms (sign test p = 0.035; Wilcoxon p = 0.0017). This is the pattern expected for lineages that predate the out-of-Africa bottleneck.

Interpretation

Most common forms are old, mostly single-lineage alleles that predate the out-of-Africa dispersal. Clock ages are hundreds of kyr. The block of 20q>3q and 3q>11p still has close relatives among native donor-arm alleles; the 6p>11p block does not.

3. Great apes (scripts/ape_forms.py, scripts/ape_junction_check.py)

Probes. For each of the 70 junction clusters with ≥5 samples, three 6 kb probes were aligned to all 240 ape chromosome ends:

  • a carrier probe centred on the switch;
  • a native own-arm probe (CHM13 A);
  • a native donor probe (CHM13 B).

Hits. In 31 of the 350 species × junction tests (17 clusters), the carrier probe aligned across the switch. All 31 were diagnosed as shared duplicon (results/ape_junction_hits_diagnosis.tsv):

  • The human carrier probe itself aligns contiguously across its switch on CHM13 A and B (e.g. 20q>3q: 0.998 on 20q, 0.993 on 3q).
  • The native probe aligns to the same ape locus with equal identity (20q>3q in chimpanzee: 0.9730 vs 0.9731).
  • The ape-side position differs from the human one (20q>3q: 109.6 kb in chimpanzee vs 35.4 kb in human).
  • No hit is at the orthologous end with ≥0.97 identity on both flanks.

These "junctions" are changes in paralog similarity inside sequence shared by both arms, not structural breakpoints. No recurrent human junction is present in a great-ape genome. The ape data therefore do not place any form before the human–chimp split, and they do not conflict with the clock ages.

Limitations of the ape test. Absence is weak evidence. The orthology of ape ends could be assigned for only 158 of 240 ends from the 300–500 kb segment (gorilla 5/48), because of large subterminal satellite and duplicon content in ape subtelomeres.

Figures

Caveats

  • Gene conversion. Subtelomeres undergo frequent interchromosomal gene conversion. Converted tracts inflate carrier diversity, so ages are upper-biased. They can also homogenise carriers with donors, so "single origin" and nesting calls are about the sequence lineage of the block, not about the mutational event.
  • Mutation rate. μ is the genome-average germline rate. Subtelomeric rates may be higher, which would make the true ages younger. Treat the absolute ages as order-of-magnitude.
  • Mixed native lineages. Native π varies from 0.18 to 4.2 SNVs/kb between donor arms. Natives can themselves be mixtures of old structural lineages, so π ratios are relative to that arm's native set, not to a population average.
  • Single reference. SNVs are called against a single CHM13 end with minimap2. Paralog misalignment in repeats survives the masking only partially.
  • Bootstrap estimator. The block-bootstrap CI of the π ratio uses a ratio-of-sums estimator, so it can exclude the mean-of-pairs point estimate when coverage is uneven (e.g. 16p>9q).
  • Labels. Superpopulation labels follow the 1000 Genomes/HPRC tables. AMR samples, and the ACB/ASW samples in AFR, are admixed.
